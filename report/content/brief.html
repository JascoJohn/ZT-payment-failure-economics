Key insight

{{anom_m}} comes from the two anomaly categories, which need containing. The remaining {{rm_m}} is steady and spans every channel; card carries {{rm_card_share}} of it, and USD card completion is where the recoverable money is.

What the data shows

  • A few accounts account for the spikes. {{an_obs}} extreme-volume observations, including one from account 47087, and that account's continuing escalation, carry {{anom_m}}. They explain {{jan_ex_share}} of January 2024's jump in failed fees.
  • Underlying failure is persistent but stable. Excluding the two anomaly categories, between {{rem_range_words}} of attempts failed in every month, with no upward trend.
  • Card is the structural problem. Excluding extreme-volume anomalies, USD card fails {{usd_card_range}} of the time, against {{mm_mk_range}} for mobile money in the same markets. The gap persists, at {{gap_gs0}} points, among customers who use both.
  • Exchange rates distort the trend. Depreciation, mostly of the cedi, cut local-currency fees by {{fx_local_eff}} in dollar terms, so constant-currency reporting is needed to see real performance.

Strategic implication. Contain the accounts in the two anomaly categories with monitoring, and invest in card completion in Kenya, Tanzania and Uganda. Treating {{failed_fees_m}} as one problem would put management attention in the wrong place.

Three actions

01
Contain the two anomaly categories

Add a relative alert (1,000+ failed attempts, 50× the account's recent median, under 5% success) and an absolute one (5,000+ failed attempts, under 5% success). Throttle and review flagged accounts in the same month.

02
Improve USD card completion in Kenya, Tanzania and Uganda

Start with merchant-months that had zero card successes, then review acceptance, routing and currency set-up. Baselines: Kenya {{rm_Kenya_r}}, Tanzania {{rm_Tanzania_r}}, Uganda {{rm_Uganda_r}} failure.

03
Report three numbers every month

Count failure rate, failed-fee share, and successful-transaction fees at constant 2022 exchange rates, each intervention against its baseline.

1. Two problems inside one headline

Every fee on a failed transaction falls into exactly one of three categories. Extreme-volume anomalies are sudden bursts of failures from single accounts. The escalation account (47087) is one account whose failures grew steadily; together they are the two anomaly categories. Remaining exposure is everything else.

3. What recovery is worth

If USD card failed no more often than mobile money in each market, the platform would have earned {{rc_base_k0}} more in successful-transaction fees over {{nm}} months, about {{rc_base_ann0}} a year. Conservative and optimistic targets give {{rc_con_k0}} and {{rc_opt_k0}}.

Read with care. These are conditional scenarios, not forecasts. They use historical volumes and observed fees, assume the gap is actually closed, and are before processing costs. Break-even is {{rc_be}} of cost per additional successful payment. Leaving the escalation account out of the mobile money benchmark raises the base case to {{bx_inc0}}.

Bottom line

A large part of the {{failed_fees_m}} comes from a small number of accounts in the two anomaly categories. The rest is a steady, long-running problem in which USD card is the main part that can be improved. Closing even part of the gap with mobile money is worth real money, and most of that opportunity sits in three markets.
