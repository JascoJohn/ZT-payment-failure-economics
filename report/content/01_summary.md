## Executive summary

<p class="lede">The Economics of Payment Failure &middot; ZT East Africa &middot; January 2022 – April 2024</p>

<p class="opening">The {{failed_fees_m}} in fees sitting on failed transactions is not one problem. It is two, and they need different responses.</p>

#### 1. The two anomaly categories: {{anom_m}} ({{anom_share}}) <span class="pill teal">Contain</span>

A small number of accounts produced sudden bursts of failed attempts, most of all in December 2023 and January 2024. {{an_obs_words}} extreme-volume observations, including one from account 47087, and that account's continuing escalation, account for most of the jump in the headline. The pattern looks like automated retries or card testing, although the data cannot confirm that. They need detecting and containing, not a product overhaul.

#### 2. Remaining exposure: {{rm_m}} ({{rm_share}}) <span class="pill amber">Improve</span>

This is everything left once the two anomaly categories are set aside. It covers every channel, but card carries {{rm_card_share}} of remaining exposure ({{rm_card_m}}) and USD card alone {{rm_usdcard_share}} of remaining exposure ({{rm_usdcard_m}}). Between {{rem_range_words}} of payment attempts failed in every one of the {{nm}} months. Excluding extreme-volume anomalies, USD card payments fail {{usd_card_range}} of the time across the {{n_markets_words}} markets, while mobile money fails {{mm_mk_range}} of the time in the same markets. Even among the {{gap_n}} customers who use both, card fails about {{gap_gs0}} percentage points more often.

#### Where the money sits

Card carries {{card_share}} of all fees on failed transactions.

#### What recovery is worth

If USD card failure came down to the mobile money level in each market, the platform would have earned about {{rc_base_k0}} more in successful-transaction fees over the {{nm}} months, roughly {{rc_base_ann0}} a year. The range is {{rc_con_k0}} (conservative) to {{rc_opt_k0}} (optimistic). This is before processing costs, it assumes the gap is actually closed, and it is not a forecast. Against a mobile money benchmark that leaves out the escalation account, the base case would be {{bx_inc0}}. Leaving out card customer-months with no successful card payment, which may be correct rejections, lowers it to {{rc_nr_k0}}, and resampling customers gives a {{rc_ci_level}} interval of {{rc_ci_lo}}–{{rc_ci_hi}}.

#### Three actions

1. **Contain the two anomaly categories.** Set up two account alerts, one for sudden spikes and one for steady escalation, so that flagged accounts are throttled and reviewed in the same month.
2. **Improve card completion in Kenya, Tanzania and Uganda.** These three markets hold {{rc_pri_k0}} of the {{rc_base_k0}} base case. Start with the merchant-months that had no successful card payments at all.
3. **Report three numbers every month:** the count failure rate, the failed-fee share, and successful-transaction fees at constant 2022 exchange rates.

#### Bottom line

A large part of the {{failed_fees_m}} comes from a small number of accounts in the two anomaly categories. The rest is a steady, long-running problem in which USD card is the main part that can be improved. Most of that opportunity sits in three markets.
